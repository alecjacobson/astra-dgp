# Independent render review — R04

Inspected `renders/r04-landing.png`, `r04-atrium_west.png`, `r04-exterior.png`, and `r03-atrium_east.png`, comparing the landing with the actual video thumbnail and the atrium with official university photographs. This review concerns those actual files; later script edits are not assumed visible in them.

The latest interiors are materially better than R01. The white light disc is gone, the east entrance has larger glazing bays, the heritage wall now reads primarily as brick, concrete is smoother, and the reduced floor seams no longer dominate. R03 east is the strongest reference-readable interior so far. R04 west presents a coherent atrium with convincing dark louvres and a clear curved-glass feature, although its proportions and bridge locations remain inferred.

## Critical next fixes

1. **Landing stair/door conflict — fix before final.** The R04 rising flight runs directly across the main door/landing approach. The thumbnail has an open, broad central landing with its ascending stair to the left. The planned move west/left is warranted. Check that the relocated stair meets an actual upper landing and retains a continuous accessible door approach; do not fix only the camera view.

2. **Floating heritage roof — fix before publishing as exterior completion.** In R04 exterior, the foreground Koffler context roof floats visibly above the tops of the walls. A deep, continuous dark gap appears along two perimeter edges. Close the shell with matching wall/parapet construction or lower the roof to meet it. The exterior shells are a major improvement, but this gap reads as broken geometry rather than an intentional cutaway.

3. **Landing floor junction — inspect geometry.** A purple-gray raised strip crosses the full landing in front of the doors. The thumbnail has a largely continuous polished walking surface. Verify that landing and gallery floor elevations align and that the strip is not a projecting fascia or riser accidentally crossing circulation. Keep guards from ending into the walking route.

4. **Landing framing/scale — adjust after stair relocation.** The foreground column occupies roughly one sixth of R04's image width, compared with roughly one tenth in the thumbnail; camera proximity amplifies the difference. Pull the camera back and reveal more open floor before changing column diameter. Thumbnail doors are a broad dark timber wall with lighter wall piers, while R04 is dominated by two tall pale piers and lighter orange timber. Darken the doors and review opening widths, but exact widths cannot be measured from this thumbnail alone.

5. **Finish the already identified shell and mapping corrections.** The upper north wing's south enclosure and uneven masonry direction remain necessary checks. R03 heritage brick is broadly improved but has unnaturally uniform horizontal banding and minimal tonal relief. Ensure brick aspect ratio is consistent around corners and reveals; no face should turn into long unbroken texture stripes.

## Secondary visual improvements

- In the landing, retain the thumbnail's dark horizontal louvred void edge at right after moving the stair. It provides recognizable depth beyond the large column.
- Give the landing's ceiling lamps visible thin frames/diffusers; current broad plain emissive rectangles read as glowing cards. This is secondary to unobstructed circulation.
- R04 west's very low-looking foreground bridge strongly blocks the upper volume. A second camera position that shows its clearance and roof structure would make the space easier to assess; do not reduce bridge thickness just to improve composition without checking geometry.
- R03 east's stair soffit remains visually serrated with exposed tread undersides. The reference presents a more continuous underside/edge at the comparable stair; a restrained concrete soffit/stringer could improve this if it preserves the actual run.

## Fidelity limits to keep explicit

The result is a reference-guided architectural approximation, not measured photogrammetry. Public video previews/thumbnail have been recovered, but they are not a decoded frame sequence at 14:52. Interior materials and identifiable motifs have direct support; full footprint, curved tower extent, roof heights, hidden rooms, exact bridge spacing, stairs and neighboring massing remain partly inferred. Current exterior is useful as schematic context, not evidence of exact exterior fidelity. A polished image should not erase those distinctions.
