# Independent architectural reference criteria

Reviewed 2026-09-16. This is a qualitative feature review following the `astra-house` render-loop assessment approach. A convincing render must preserve architectural relationships, not merely contain many objects. Dimensions and hidden construction remain estimates unless separately measured.

## Priority geometry and materials

| Priority | Feature | Evidence / render criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Long, tall, narrow public atrium | Render an axial view with clearly different long sides, daylight end wall, upper circulation and bridge/stair crossings. Avoid treating this as a generic large room. |
| 1 | Preserved yellow-brick heritage exterior within modern atrium | `events-3`, `university-2`: tall rectangular window openings, brick pilasters and relief, pale stone base/sills; several windows have dark grille bars. Adjacent modern side has exposed concrete and dark louvres. |
| 1 | Round exposed concrete columns | Tall cylindrical shafts have visible ring construction joints; columns stand free of walls and align with circulation. Use actual round geometry and warm gray, slightly varied concrete. |
| 1 | Dark horizontal wood louvre screens | `events-4`, `events-6`: dense, thin horizontal dark-brown slats over broad upper-storey bays, held in vertical dark members; visible depth and gaps are essential. These are not solid brown panels. |
| 1 | Stair, bridge and frosted guards | Thumbnail: stairs at left, pale blue frosted glass panels in gray metal frames, dark continuous wood handrails, polished landing and a round column to right. Render a thumbnail-like landing view to judge this directly. |
| 2 | Curved suspended glass volume | `events-4`, `events-5`: large upper-level curved glazing wall/volume with dense vertical divisions and dark perimeter edging. It must visibly project over circulation, not become a flat curtain wall. |
| 2 | Skylit roof and glazed ends | `events-3`: repeated white roof beams and sloping glazed strips; tall end glazing grid above door banks. This determines atrium light direction and vertical scale. |
| 2 | Floor and balconies | Gray terrazzo floor with fine black/white aggregate and modest reflection; exposed concrete balcony edges, gray steel framing, glass railings, limited purple-gray fascia. Avoid mirror floors and uniformly opaque cyan glazing. |
| 2 | Street facade | `university-3`: cream brick broad planar mass, inset and projecting green-blue glazed bays with thin gray mullions, darker glazed base, entrance steps. Massing should read as assembled intersecting volumes. |
| 2 | Incorporated gabled house | `university-4`: narrow pale-brick historic gabled frontage with red brick ornamental bands, arched upper openings, steep gray gable roof, door numbered 44. It is surrounded closely by modern cream masonry and glazing. |
| 3 | Small scale cues | Timber stools/tables, dark wooden doors, restrained signs/posters, downlights, continuous timber bench beside void guard. Add after proportions and circulation read correctly. |

## Minimum useful render set

1. Eye-level atrium axis: heritage masonry opposite modern louvres, full-height columns, bridge and roof daylight visible.
2. Landing/stair view: thumbnail comparison emphasizing frosted guards, column size, handrails, doors, and polished floor.
3. Opposite atrium axis: curved glass upper volume and broad modern-side louvres.
4. Exterior three-quarter view: cream masonry, glass bays, street entrances and incorporated heritage mass.

Each iteration should identify the reference image, render camera, three most consequential mismatches, the actual geometry/material changes, and a follow-up image. Judge proportions and spatial relationships before finish. Do not label the result photogrammetry or a measured reconstruction without evidence. Publish authored renders separately from third-party photographs and retain source credits.
